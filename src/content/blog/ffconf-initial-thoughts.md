It's two days since I was at ffconf in Brighton. I went last year and it was a really cool experience. This year it was a cool... and different experience.

One of the things I like about ffconf is that it isn't a purely technical conference. No, scratch that, it's just not a technical conference. But it's one of those things that we actually need as people who work in this field. ~~I'm a little taken aback at how long~~ I don't know when I'll be able to process all of it. 

I was thinking about how to write about this, maybe going through each of the talks and giving my thoughts, but that seems boring. All of the talks were good. The opening talk by <a href="https://nielsleenheer.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Niels Leenheer</a> was fantastic. Mainly it was about doing Doom in CSS but for me it was the first part that was more intriguing. Getting an oscilloscope to display moving images via CSS by running it through the web audio API was super-cool. That's the kind of thing that is like, how the f\*\*k did his mind go in the directions that it did. 

I think the last two talks are what are sticking with me. The talk <a href="https://cennydd.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Cennydd Bowles</a> gave, I might need to wait for the video to come out to re-watch it, was a sobering and probably necessary thing to hear in light of the conversations we're having about LLMs and our place in the world. It was, by his description, fairly pessimistic, and I feel like I mostly agree with his points. Again, I might need to re-watch it to give specifics. I think Cennydd's advocacy for some type of unionisation of software is probably on the money. It's interesting to hear some of the comments around this after being in a profession that was said to be doomed for all of the time I was in it (classical music). This was one of his points too. For years we've turned the power of technology, in the name of disruption - or whatever other buzzword was appropriate at the time - to other fields and said "get ready for the future". Now we're getting the same treatment.

The last talk by <a href="https://www.lenareinhard.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Lena Reinhard</a> gave me a bit of optimism to take with me. Again, there was a lot to take in, and this is another one of those talks I'll need to re-watch, but in quick summary it pushed back on the "this is all inevitable" narrative. That we've spent years taking friction out of our lives and whether that's actually a good thing.

Well, the kids are about to wake up so I'll wrap this up for now.
