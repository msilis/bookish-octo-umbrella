I watched a commentary video discussing another video posted by an AI influencer-type person. In one part of the video, the influencer-type person, while they're discussing the looping agents they're using, said that the next step for them is to automate the idea phase as well. Or at least, as they put it, make it as frictionless as possible.

I was just putting away some clothes and this thought hit me:

Removing humans from the loop, especially the idea part, I just have one question. Why? 

In software engineering we're building things for humans to use. Fine, even if you believe in a future where everyone interacts with the internet via a voice-prompted AI, at some point a human will have to use that piece of software. But even at a basic level, reading the code or not reading the code, you should be involved in the friction of making the thing. 

Is there a saying such as, 'if everything is easy, nothing is easy'? Just had to do a quick Google for it, and I didn't find anything. What do I mean by 'if everything is easy, nothing is easy'? Not sure. I'll stew on that for a bit.

But I think the friction is probably an important point. If there is no friction, there's no thinking. There's no thought about tradeoffs regarding features. There's no thought put to how you'd want to be able to navigate through whatever you're building. There's no effort to try. There is no effort. 

So what becomes the hard thing? Or why does it become hard if the process is easy? What's the point of doing anything, really?

I know most of these kinds of statements (like the ones the influencer person said) are just said for clicks and engagement. I was almost going to say, well, there will be someone who believes them and tries to replicate their workflow in their own project. But, I think even then, friction evens out the playing field. 

What does that mean? 

I mean, even if you have a half-baked idea or just delegate everything to an LLM, the friction of getting people to use it will deter most of these kinds of projects from gaining traction. That kind of friction will make sure they're weeded out.

Back to the humans in the loop. 
If you build something, or sorry, your agent builds something, and you have no part of it, who does it exist for? This brings up another article that basically stated, if you expect me to read something, have the decency or respect of my time to have written it yourself. The same could be said here. If you want me to use a service, have the decency to have put some thought into the user experience and the feel of the application. If you don't need to be involved in any part of its creation, does it really need to be done? Does it really need to exist? I don't know, maybe I'm off base here.
