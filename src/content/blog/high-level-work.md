"AI will do the low-level work to free you up to do high-value work"



I'm paraphrasing a quote that was mentioned in a talk as a, I don't quite remember, argument let's say for the positive use cases of LLMs. And I'll say LLMs because when most people talk about this, this is the stuff they mean. Agents/LLMs doing the grunt work.



The counterargument was: if there was high-value work to do in your job, you'd already be doing it.



This made me think: is there really such a thing as low-value/high-value or does the former inform the latter? 



This also reminded me of the argument that all of that seemingly menial work was actually quite important to the job as a whole. I'm not talking about any one particular industry here, just in general terms. You were informed, by the busy tasks, in the big decisions that you'd sometimes have to make. You had a better understanding of the problem if your brain had a chance to wander while you were filling in that spreadsheet.



I think the other thing that the quote doesn't take into account is the fact that no human can be switched on for 8 hours a day doing all of the "high-value" work, for days on end without burning out. It gives the impression that we're capable of sustained focus, of endless mental stamina, to only be working on those important tasks. I'd have to look up some statistics here but I just don't think that's the case.



Side-note: everyone seems to be going to speaking to LLMs instead of typing, because it's faster. But you know what? You know what typing does? You need to make tiny decisions, about the next work, the next idea that you don't have to while you're talking. Sure, you can 'ramble' while typing as well, but it's really not the same. 



I'm a fan of writing things down. I'm a fan of writing things down so I don't have to keep them in my head. I found that out while trying to deal with the way my brain used to go into overdrive. I'd be really fixated on a thought just so I don't forget it. Then, one day I decided, because I couldn't sleep, or couldn't focus on something else, to just write it down to get it out of my head. And that seemed to work.



So writing is thinking. Sure, you do have to think to talk but the whole thing is more ephemeral. 



Does this all tie together somehow? I'm not sure... see I went on a bit of a writing ramble there.
