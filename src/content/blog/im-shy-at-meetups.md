I'm shy at meetups. I've gone to more than a handful at this point but I'm not going to be the one to start a conversation. So sometimes I go with a colleague I used to work with. And those are great, we chat about what we're doing and the state of things. All the normal stuff.



I was just at one tonight. We ended up talking to another developer afterwards and our experiences using LLMs, agents, whatever. He had just gotten laid of from a role he was at for five years and was going through what his job search was like. He said something interesting that I did not agree with. I'm paraphrasing, but it was something like, "I don't see a difference between myself and someone just out of university when it comes to software development". This was referencing him going into the job search with 12 years of experience. 



I told him I didn't think that was right. I'll tell you I don't think that's right. I don't have 12 years of experience in this field to form that opinion, but I do have what I have seen working in the field for the last little while. 



Ok, so even if you're heavily leveraging LLMs in your workflows, and sure anyone at this point can prompt their way to a complete project (I hesitate to say product)... My point was that those years of experience, even if you're only sitting there approving changes, inform you on what to push back on, what to explore further, what to... I don't know, but you get the picture. I think it's still naive at this point to think that experience doesn't count for anything. Even if you think model with at some point get to a mythical level where they have stopped hallucinating and don't make any mistakes, we're not there yet. Sure, there's also a debate whether we'll get there with the current technology. 



I was a little taken aback when I heard him say it. I just don't see it that way. But maybe you do, and hey, that's fine too. We're all just trying to get by the best we can.
