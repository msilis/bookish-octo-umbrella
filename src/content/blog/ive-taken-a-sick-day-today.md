This was something that would have not been a no-brainer decision a while back. When I was teaching, taking a sick day meant a lot of things. If it was my own students, that would potentially mean lost income. Or it would mean make-up lessons. Lost income in another way.

If it was during on of my in-school days it would mean that my colleagues day just got harder. Any plans they had for their own students would have gone out the window as they would need to take mine as well. 

There was quite a bit of guilt associated with this on my end. I think I'd built up the idea that I needed to be seen as a responsible person, that I could be counted on. Taking a day off meant the opposite. I still grapple with this in a way but working in a job where you're supposed to take care of yourself if you're ill is a no-brainer. If i'm in the office, no one else wants to catch what I have. If I'm working from home, the mere fact that I'm sitting at my desk means that my recovery takes longer. The question changes from "why do you need to take a sick day?" to "why haven't you taken a sick day?".

Weird to say that there's a freedom in that. A freedom and permission to rest and heal. Yeah, that's weird.
