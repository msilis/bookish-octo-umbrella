This is a quote from <a href="https://aiandeducation.mit.edu/report/#27-augmentation-not-automation" rel="noopener noreferrer">MIT's Ad Hoc Committee on AI Use in Teaching, Learning, and Research Training</a>:

"*As AI-enabled technologies become more capable, it will be possible – and tempting – to offload more “thinking” tasks to them; not surprisingly, students told us the temptation was greatest when they feared they would miss a deadline. There are early signals, however, that overreliance on chatbots can have a range of significant negative consequences – diminishing critical thinking, weakening memory, eroding confidence, and undermining mastery*"

It's interesting reading this because I have totally experienced this. I've been very conscious about how much I let Claude do on its own. I'm not interested in going fast except when it comes to the end of the day for some reason. I'll be working on a feature and it might hit 4PM. I'll start delegating more and more to Claude to see if I can finish things up. 

There's a part of me that knows whats going on here, the trade that I'm making for getting one more bit of the feature done. That's the part that feels icky. Sure, I might be going faster but it's at the expense of the four points mentioned in the quote's last sentence: critical thinking, memory, confidence, and mastery. 

My go-to for the moment has been to remind myself that tomorrow is another day. I'm fortunate to work in a place that doesn't enforce hard deadlines on me. I have the space I need to get the work done. Of course, if it's a little faster, then all the better, but that's not the only or most important metric being measured. 

The whole MIT report is a real interesting read. Education is definitely one of the places the last four years have hit the hardest.
